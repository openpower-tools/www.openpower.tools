# Timeline, playhead and linked-view UX: state of the art 2024 to 2026

Facet 3 of 4 for the opt-chart design. Every source cited below was fetched and read on 2026-09-03; where a page could not be fetched or did not contain the claim, the finding says "not found". Direct quotations are marked with quotation marks; everything else is paraphrase.

## Summary

- The trace viewers we checked (Chrome DevTools Performance, Playwright Trace Viewer, Cypress, Replay.io, Perfetto, WebPageTest) converge on one grammar: hover previews the nearest frame, snapshot or screenshot without committing; click seeks, selects or pins; drag selects a time range that filters the sibling panels; and the cursor is a full-height vertical rule that shows in every view at once.
- Dashboards (Grafana, Kibana, Datadog) synchronise the cursor across panels by default and treat synchronised tooltips as an opt-in, because tooltips on every panel are noise and a legend or axis readout can carry the value.
- The cleanest model for a view that follows a clock and emits intent is Observable Plot's pointer: the chart sets element.value and dispatches input; the custom-element checklist adds "dispatch events for internal activity, never in response to a host-set property, and never reflect a high-frequency property such as currentTime".
- Current-time labels sit on the axis (Plot crosshair) or adjacent to the thumb (ARIA seek slider), never over the series; values at the cursor go to a legend (uPlot, Kibana TSVB) or to automatic peak and trough labels (Datadog).
- Annotation practice (Datawrapper, FT, Tufte, Vega): label directly instead of a legend, use two text levels, outline text that sits on gridlines, never rotate labels, and resolve collisions by dropping or deferring labels (parity or greedy) or by moving them into a numbered key.
- Chapters are ticks in the track whose title appears as the scrubber crosses them; YouTube's most-replayed graph appears above the bar only while seeking and is otherwise invisible.
- Frame stepping conventions are stable across editors: Left/Right one frame, Shift+arrow a larger jump (ten frames in Frame.io, one second in Resolve), JKL shuttle with repeated presses raising the rate, and comma and period while paused on YouTube.
- Pending seek with an explicit cancel is an established pattern (YouTube precise seeking with Play or Cancel, release-to-cancel on mobile); our press-drag, commit-on-release, Escape-to-cancel already matches it, so the work is in the details (snap-back, touch, labels), not the model.
- WCAG 2.3.3 exempts only essential motion: a playhead moving to the current time is essential; thumb flights, ghost travel and page-turn easing are not and must collapse under prefers-reduced-motion.
- Minimal contract for opt-chart: for= names the clock; time, duration, pending and peek are properties (not reflected attributes); opt-chart-peek and opt-chart-seek are the only outgoing intents; custom states following, peeking, scrubbing let ::part styling react without script.

## Findings

1. Chrome DevTools Performance panel (https://developer.chrome.com/docs/devtools/performance/reference, accessed 2026-09-03).
   - Hover: "Shows you a vertical marker that spans the entire performance trace when you hover over the Timeline overview" and "Highlights a range in the Timeline overview when you hover over items in the Main track." With the Screenshots checkbox on (a screenshot of every frame), "Hover over the Timeline overview to view a screenshot of how the page looked during that moment of the recording"; the overview is the strip with the CPU, FPS and NET charts.
   - Selection: click, hold and drag across the overview selects a portion; keyboard W, A, S, D zoom in, move left, zoom out, move right; two-finger trackpad swipes do the same. Selecting a portion and clicking its "N ms" button expands it to fill the overview and starts a breadcrumb chain; breadcrumbs nest while the range is over 5 ms and clicking one jumps back to that zoom level. Holding Shift while dragging shows "how long that portion took" at the bottom of the selection (the example reads 488.53 ms).
   - Marks: FCP, LCP, DCL and L are vertical lines across the whole trace; "Hover over the marker names at the bottom of the trace to see their timestamp." performance.mark() shows as an individual mark with a tooltip, performance.measure() as a yellow span on the Timings track.
   - Frames: hover a frame in the Frames section for a tooltip; frames are colour-coded idle (white), rendered (green), partially presented (yellow with a sparse dash pattern) and dropped (red with a dense pattern); clicking a frame outlines it in blue and shows a small screenshot in the Summary tab that zooms on click. Long tasks carry a red triangle with the part beyond 50 ms shaded red; the selected event is outlined in blue.

4. Replay.io (https://docs.replay.io/basics/replay-devtools/browser-devtools/replay-viewer, https://docs.replay.io/basics/replay-devtools/time-travel-devtools/focus-window and https://blog.replay.io/how-we-designed-our-focus-mode, accessed 2026-09-03).

6. Grafana (https://grafana.com/docs/grafana/latest/dashboards/build-dashboards/modify-dashboard-settings/ and https://grafana.com/docs/grafana/latest/dashboards/build-dashboards/annotate-visualizations/, accessed 2026-09-03).

7. Kibana and Datadog.
   - Kibana docs (https://www.elastic.co/docs/explore-analyze/dashboards/create-dashboard, accessed 2026-09-03): "Sync cursor across panels: When you hover your cursor over a time series chart or a heatmap, the cursor on all other related dashboard charts automatically appears. Sync tooltips across panels: When you hover your cursor over a Lens chart, the tooltips on all other related dashboard charts automatically appear."
   - Defaults and reasoning (https://github.com/elastic/kibana/pull/143355 and https://github.com/elastic/kibana/issues/113719, accessed 2026-09-03): the sync-cursor switch is "by default on" for backwards compatibility and turning it off also disables tooltip sync; it exists "especially for dashboards with performance issues". In the tooltip discussion, the charts lead argued the option "should be off by default" because of "the high increase of noise in the dashboard UX where suddenly all the charts now are showing tooltip flags all-around", listed three levels (no sync; cursor only, "the current chart will show the tooltip, all the others just the cursor"; cursor plus tooltip everywhere) and noted that in TSVB and Timelion "the legend already reflects the value of the current cursor". The reporter's counterpoint: "Without the popups, the best a viewer can do is visually guess at the likely Y axis value for a given point." The same thread records a rendering defect, the synced cursor "hidden behind the grid line", which is a z-order lesson for our playhead.
   - Datadog (https://docs.datadoghq.com/dashboards/graph_insights/correlations/ and https://docs.datadoghq.com/dashboards/widgets/timeseries/, accessed 2026-09-03): "Similar to dashboards, hovering over a graph creates a time-synced line on all other graphs", and the correlation search window is set by "Click and drag on the graph to set the time frame". Timeseries widgets show automatic labels "on notable datapoints, such as peaks and troughs, so you can read key values without hovering over the graph"; with grouped or aliased queries "Datadog labels the last point of each line with the series name instead"; "a maximum of three labels per series, and doesn't show labels at all if the graph has more than six series". Event overlays draw deploys and similar events on the time axis, markers add horizontal lines or ranges with a label at the bottom left, and the last incomplete interval is shaded and labelled "partial data".

8. Chart libraries: pointer, crosshair, brush and sync.
   - Observable Plot pointer (https://observablehq.com/plot/interactions/pointer, accessed 2026-09-03): the pointer transform "filters a mark interactively such that only the point closest to the pointer is rendered"; pointerX and pointerY are for charts with a "dominant" dimension such as time, and "consider distance in both dimensions, but the distance along the non-dominant dimension is divided by 100", so y only breaks ties between series; the default reach is 40 px (maxRadius), which can leave dead spots on large marks; "clicking on the chart locks the currently-focused point until you click again" (click-to-stick); the transform "emits an input event whenever the focused points changes, and sets the value of the plot element", so a plot can be used as a form input; several pointer transforms should share target positions so the same point is focused across marks.
   - Plot crosshair and tip (https://observablehq.com/plot/interactions/crosshair and https://observablehq.com/plot/marks/tip, accessed 2026-09-03): the crosshair "shows the x and y value of the point closest to the pointer on the bottom and left sides of the frame", drawn "just outside" the frame "typically on top of the axes", with a rule at 0.2 opacity and a white text stroke of width 5 "to improve legibility"; it has no format option yet (issue 1596). The tip mark chooses an anchor automatically from a preferred side, pools so "at most one tip will be visible at a time", and may need overflow: visible to avoid truncation at the frame edge.
   - uPlot (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leeoniya/uPlot/master/README.md and https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leeoniya/uPlot/master/demos/sync-cursor.html, accessed 2026-09-03): the README lists "Cursor sync for multiple charts", "Focus closest series" and "Legend with live values" as features, and as deliberate non-features "No transitions or animations - they're always pure distractions", "No collision avoidance for axis tick labels" and no built-in drag panning "due to ambiguous native zoom/selection behavior". The demo creates uPlot.sync("moo"), subscribes and unsubscribes charts, and configures cursor: {lock: true, focus: {prox: 16}, sync: {key, setSeries: true, match: [matchScaleKeys, matchScaleKeys], filters: {pub: upDownFilter}}}; the filter drops mouseup and mousedown from the broadcast, and a second example matches series by label so charts with mismatched series order still focus the same series. The docs README carries no cursor section (marked WIP).
   - Vega-Lite selections (https://vega.github.io/vega-lite/docs/selection.html, accessed 2026-09-03): a parameter becomes a selection with type "point" (first value on click, more toggled with shift-click) or "interval" (a continuous range on drag); on can be changed, for example to pointerover; clear defaults to dblclick; nearest: true uses an invisible Voronoi so "moving the mouse cursor back-and-forth snaps the vertical rule and label to the nearest date value"; intervals draw a rectangle (fill #333333 at 0.125 opacity) that can be translated by dragging inside it and resized with the wheel; resolve is global, union or intersect across facets or repeated views.
   - d3-brush (https://d3js.org/d3-brush, accessed 2026-09-03): drag on the overlay defines a new selection, drag on the selection translates it, handles move an edge; META starts a new brush anywhere, ALT resizes around the centre, SPACE locks the size; brush.move can snap a selection to semantic boundaries with a transition; listeners receive start, brush and end events with selection, sourceEvent and mode (drag, space, handle, center).

9. Custom-element data flow (https://web.dev/articles/custom-elements-best-practices, accessed 2026-09-03): "Dispatch events in response to internal component activity" and "Do not dispatch events in response to the host setting a property (downward data flow)", because the host "knows the current state because it just set it" and doing so "may cause infinite loops with data binding systems". Primitive attributes and properties should stay in sync, "You shouldn't reflect high frequency properties, e.g. currentTime in a video player"; rich data (objects, arrays) should be accepted only as properties; state should be expressed as attributes, not self-applied classes; author-set globals such as tabindex and role must not be overridden.

13. Accessibility and touch sliders.
   - ARIA slider pattern (https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/slider/, accessed 2026-09-03): Right and Up increase by one step, Left and Down decrease, Home and End go to the first and last value, Page Up and Page Down (optional) move "by an amount larger than the step change"; role slider with aria-valuenow, aria-valuemin, aria-valuemax and aria-valuetext when the raw value "is not user-friendly"; focus goes on the thumb; the pattern warns that touch-based assistive technologies may not yet synthesise the key events sliders need.
   - Media seek example (https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/slider/examples/slider-seek/, accessed 2026-09-03): value is seconds, aria-valuetext converts it to minutes and seconds; "since repeating the maximum value every time the slider value changes is potentially distracting, the maximum value is included in aria-valuetext only when the slider is initialized and when the thumb loses keyboard focus"; "The display of the slider's current value remains adjacent to the thumb as the thumb is moved, so people with a small field of view (e.g., due to magnification) can easily see the value"; Page Up and Down move 15 steps; the role sits on the SVG g that is the thumb, the rail rect is aria-hidden, the svg is role none, and borders use currentcolor with forced-color-adjust: auto so high-contrast modes keep the rail and thumb visible.
   - WCAG 2.3.3 (https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html, accessed 2026-09-03): "Motion animation triggered by interaction can be disabled, unless the animation is essential to the functionality or the information being conveyed"; essential means removal "would fundamentally change the information or functionality"; sufficient techniques are C39 (prefers-reduced-motion in CSS), SCR40 (the same query in JavaScript) and a site-wide preference; the example of essential animation is previewing an animation in an authoring tool.
   - Value labels: material-web (https://github.com/material-components/material-web/blob/main/docs/components/slider.md, accessed 2026-09-03) says "A value label displays the specific value that corresponds with the handle's placement. The label appears when the handle is selected" (attribute labeled), and its input event bubbles and is composed while change bubbles only. Apple HIG Sliders (https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sliders, accessed 2026-09-03): minimum on the leading side, "Consider supplementing a slider with a corresponding text field and stepper", macOS tick marks with labels only where needed ("labeling only the minimum and maximum values is sufficient"), "Consider giving live feedback as the value of a slider changes", and "provide a tooltip that displays the value of the thumb when people hold their pointer over it".

14. Annotation, labelling and collision practice.
   - Datawrapper text guidance (https://blog.datawrapper.de/text-in-data-visualizations/, accessed 2026-09-03, Lisa Charlotte Muth 2022): "remove the color key and directly label your categories" (the default for line charts on desktop; on mobile the key moves to the top); repeat units in axis labels, tooltips and annotations; move axis ticks to the side where the important data is; annotate design elements such as "a highlight range", outliers and causes; body text sans-serif, regular, over 12 px; limit hierarchy to two levels ("a 12px gray and a 14px black") and emphasise with bold; "Don't center-align your text"; "Don't make your readers turn their heads" (no rotated labels); "If your text sits on other elements, even just a subtle gridline, consider using a text outline" in the background colour.
   - Datawrapper annotations (https://www.datawrapper.de/academy/how-to-create-text-annotations, accessed 2026-09-03): click places an annotation with an arrow, drag places a text box; a nine-point anchor grid and pixel offsets keep an annotation the same distance from its data point at any width; "Overlapping or cropped annotations are annoying for you and your readers. To make sure your readers can actually decipher your annotations even on mobile devices, they get numbered and move below the chart by default"; in Multiple Lines an annotation can repeat in every panel.
   - Datawrapper small multiples (https://www.datawrapper.de/blog/what-to-consider-when-creating-small-multiple-line-charts and https://www.datawrapper.de/academy/customizing-your-small-multiple-line-chart, accessed 2026-09-03): shrink panels, choose fewer panels, keep annotations short ("U.S. highest in Q3"), use colour to categorise rather than distinguish, sort panels by start, end, difference, change, range or title, repeat all lines in the background of each panel for comparison, and make independent y scales obvious. Custom ticks: "Datawrapper makes sure that labels don't overlap, so not all labels will show up if space doesn't permit"; "By default, the grid labels (e.g. your years) are only shown in panels of the first column"; "By default, tooltips are turned on and synced in all panels", with a switch to turn the sync off.
   - FT practice (https://lab.imedd.org/en/from-data-to-storytelling-concept-and-design-tips-from-the-financial-times-john-burn-murdoch/, accessed 2026-09-03): a cited study compared four variants (data and axes only, a text paragraph, a titled lightly annotated chart, a fully annotated titled chart) and "the minimalist charts had ranked lowest"; eye tracking found a Z path from title to axes to data; text is what people remember; Burn-Murdoch moved labels "directly onto the lines" so readers stop "checking back and forth between the legend and the lines"; "Minimize distraction. Maximize contrast."
   - Tufte sparklines (https://www.edwardtufte.com/notebook/sparkline-theory-and-practice-edward-tufte/, accessed 2026-09-03): "A sparkline is a small intense, simple, word-sized graphic with typographic resolution"; "Data graphics should have the resolution of typography"; "General idea = max[data], min[design]"; "Should have design minimization, not data minimization."
   - Vega axis collision options (https://vega.github.io/vega/docs/axes/, accessed 2026-09-03): labelOverlap false by default; true or "parity" removes every other label ("works well for standard linear axes"); "greedy" performs "a linear scan of the labels ... removing any label that overlaps with the last visible label" (better for log scales); labelSeparation sets the minimum gap between label boxes; labelBound hides labels that exceed the axis range; labelFlush and labelFlushOffset align the end labels inward; labelLimit truncates; tickMinStep and interval-based tickCount control density on time scales.

## Recommendations for opt-chart and opt-film

Priority 1, contract and sync semantics

- One clock, one direction. opt-film keeps ownership of time and broadcasts opt-film-time on every render (film.rs:405-413, a composed bubbling CustomEvent whose detail is the time in seconds as a bare number). An opt-chart with for= renders the received time and never moves its own playhead in response to its own seek event; the film applies the seek and the next broadcast moves every view together. Reasoning: web.dev's "downward data flow" rule and Plot's input model both avoid the double-update and loop hazards of two-way binding, and Kibana's and uPlot's sync buses are single-source for the same reason.
- Outgoing intents, both composed and bubbling: opt-chart-peek with detail {time} while hovering or during a pending drag and {time: null} when the pointer leaves; opt-chart-seek with detail {time} on release, click or key. Optionally opt-chart-range with {start, end} or null for an interval brush (see below). Keep the detail an object with a time field rather than a bare number so it can grow (source, index of nearest sample) without breaking listeners; the existing opt-film-time bare-number detail should migrate the same way when opt-chart lands, or opt-chart should accept both.
- Properties, not reflected attributes, for time, duration, peek and pending (web.dev: do not reflect currentTime). Attributes only for for=, and for static configuration such as a sample step used by the keyboard.
- Custom states via ElementInternals: following (a clock is attached), peeking (a peek is showing), scrubbing (a pending seek is in flight). opt-film already exposes playing, pending and peeking (film.rs:19-21, :487, :587); opt-chart should use the same names where the meaning is the same so page CSS and the interaction report treat both alike. Uncertain: whether a Plot-style click-to-stick lock is worth a state at all; in every player checked, click seeks, so a lock would need a modifier (for example Shift+click) or should be dropped. Recommendation: drop it for now.
- Sync levels, following Kibana and Grafana defaults: the cursor (peek line and playhead) is always synchronised across chart, strip, stage and machine token; value readouts appear only on the view under the pointer. Do not add floating tooltips to every synced view; put the value at the cursor in the series' direct label or an axis readout.

Priority 2, hover and scrub ergonomics

- Peek with pointerX semantics: snap to the nearest sample (chart) or frame (strip) along x within a reach of about 40 px, ignore y, and carry the snapped time in the peek event so strip, stage and chart show the same frame. This is the Playwright film-strip and DevTools overview behaviour, and Plot's justification (dead spots vanish, small marks stay hoverable) applies to our sampled series.
- Keep the pending seek exactly as it is (press, drag to choose, release commits, Escape cancels; film.rs:576-602). Add YouTube's release-to-cancel: when the pointer returns within a few pixels of the press origin, show a "release to cancel" state and cancel on release. This is an extrapolation from a mobile-only feature to pointer drags, so treat it as an experiment rather than settled practice.
- Keys. The current set (film.rs:1030-1094: Space or K, comma and period one frame, arrows five frames, J and L ten frames, Home and End, digits for tenths, less-than and greater-than for speed, Escape) already matches YouTube for comma, period, J, L, Home, End and digits, and matches Frame.io and Resolve in spirit (arrows a small step, a modifier a larger one). Add Shift+arrow as the larger jump (ten samples or one second, whichever we pick, but pick one and document it) and Page Up and Page Down as the ARIA larger step; consider Ctrl or Alt plus arrow for chapter jumps as YouTube does. Keep aria-valuetext short: the current text "t seconds, frame k of n, chapter" (film.rs:437-447) repeats the total on every step, which the ARIA seek example avoids by naming the maximum only on init and blur.
- Touch: there is no hover, so peek must not be the only way to preview. Press-and-hold on the chart or strip should begin the pending seek with the preview above the finger (the .peek box already sits above the chart at film.rs:745), and the label must not rely on hover to reveal a value. Do not adopt the iOS slide-down speed gesture; it is undocumented by Apple and its own users report the thumb hides the readout.

Priority 3, rendering conventions for the pure Rust renderer

- Current-time label: draw it on the time axis at the playhead x (Plot crosshair convention) with a paint-order stroke in the background colour, and beside the thumb while scrubbing (ARIA seek example). film.rs:430-433 currently places head-t inside the plot area to the right of the playhead, where it can sit over a series; move it to the axis band. Give the playhead and peek line a z-order above the grid (the Kibana defect).
- Marks: vertical rules with the label along the bottom edge of the plot (DevTools markers, Grafana annotation icons at the bottom). Apply greedy collision removal with a minimum separation (Vega labelOverlap "greedy" with labelSeparation): keep the label of the mark nearest the playhead, drop the rest, and let a dropped label reappear when the pointer or the playhead is on its rule. film.rs:220-232 draws every chapter label at the top right of its rule with no overlap handling; two close chapters will collide today.
- Chapters: keep the ticks in the progress bar (film.rs:273-280 already draws 2 by 10 px ticks, the YouTube convention) and show the chapter title in the time readout as the playhead crosses it (film.rs:402 and :445 already resolve the chapter with chapter_at). Do not also print chapter names along the axis; that is the collision case above.
- Series: direct labels at a chosen fraction of the series are already there (film.rs:250-259, the Datawrapper and FT practice). Add a background-coloured outline (paint-order: stroke) to series labels and mark labels so they read over gridlines.
- Band and range: keep the band as a low-opacity rect with an outlined label. If a range selection is added, use Shift+drag (DevTools) to avoid fighting the press-drag pending seek, draw it like d3's selection rect with two draggable handles, print the range duration under the selection, and emit opt-chart-range so the interaction report can filter sampled properties to the range (Playwright's filtering model). This is lower priority than everything above.
- Reduced motion: the playhead jumping to the new time is essential under WCAG 2.3.3 and stays; travelling markers on the strip, thumb flights, ghost travel and page-turn easing are not essential and should become instant under prefers-reduced-motion, using the site's motion tokens. uPlot's stance ("no transitions or animations") is a reasonable default for the chart itself: animate nothing inside the SVG except the playhead position.
- Small multiples: when several opt-charts follow one clock, draw the time axis labels once (Datawrapper: first column only) and sync only the cursor; the second and later charts hide their axis labels via a part or attribute.

Dissent and uncertainty

- Synced tooltips: Kibana's reporter wanted synced value popups; the charts team judged them noise. For our few-series charts a per-series value in the direct label under the cursor may satisfy both; this is untested.
- Range selection: Playwright, DevTools and Perfetto all offer it, but none of them also uses drag for seeking. Adding it to a chart that already scrubs on press-drag requires a modifier and risks confusing the two gestures; ship only if the interaction report needs it.
- Hover-nearest-frame on the strip: WebPageTest and Lighthouse use fixed-interval thumbnails rather than nearest-frame lookup; our strip is per-frame, so nearest-sample snapping is the better fit, but the strip's page turns must not move under a pending drag.
- Netflix, Apple TV and Apple HIG Playback could not be fetched, so any claim about their hover previews would be from memory and is deliberately omitted.

## Open questions for the other researchers

- API facet: should series, marks, bands and chapters arrive as a light-DOM table or JSON script (accessible fallback, as opt-film does today) or as rich properties (web.dev advises properties for objects)? Who owns chapter data when a film and a chart are both on the page, and does the chart derive chapters from the machine's marks as the film does?
- API facet: should the opt-film-time detail become an object {time, duration, playing} so opt-chart can render the played bar and the end without a second attribute, and can that change be made without breaking opt-machine?
- Accessibility facet: role=slider on the whole chart (current) versus a dedicated thumb part carrying the role as the ARIA seek example does; how should marks, bands and chapters be exposed (a list, aria-describedby, or only in valuetext); should the live region announce chapter changes during playback?
- Rendering facet: label collision needs text metrics inside Rust; is a ch-based width estimate plus paint-order stroke acceptable, or should the wasm side measure once with getComputedTextLength and re-render? Do the theme contrast tests cover the playhead, peek line and outlined labels over series colours in both themes, and is z-order (playhead above grid) enforced by the renderer rather than by CSS?
- Touch and input facet: gate hover peek on pointer: fine and provide press-and-hold on coarse pointers; does the interaction report tool drive touch input through CDP so the touch path is exercised?
