# opt-chart research, facet 2: accessibility and colour

Facet: accessibility and colour for data charts, state of the art 2024 to 2026. Every cited page was fetched and read on 2026-09-03; bibliography entries marked [excerpt] were read through search-result excerpts of the page rather than a full fetch. Figures marked "computed here" are not from a source: they come from the scripts in /tmp/chart-research/src (refit.py to refit4.py, run with `uv run --with colour-science --with numpy python refit3.py`). Those scripts use the WCAG relative-luminance formula from the Understanding documents, Oklab matrices cross-checked against the published inverse coefficients and against colour-science to four decimals, CIEDE2000 and Machado 2009 simulation at severity 1.0 from colour-science, and the APCA-W3 0.0.98G-4g constants as transcribed in a third-party Rust port. Treat the computed numbers as reproducible estimates, not measurements.

## Summary

- SC 1.4.11 in a line chart: each series line, and each gridline you need in order to read values, is a graphical object that must reach 3:1 against the adjacent surface; lines need not contrast with each other unless they overlap; visible values, direct labels or a data table remove the requirement from the shapes. The Understanding text explicitly warns that anti-aliasing makes thin lines fainter and tells authors to avoid them or exceed 3:1.
- WCAG 3 (Working Draft, 3 March 2026) still names no contrast algorithm. APCA's author has a preliminary non-text table tying required Lc to stroke width (1 px Lc 90, 1.5 px Lc 75, 2 px Lc 60, 3 px Lc 50) and says 1 px lines can lose Lc 30 to anti-aliasing. Computed on our surfaces: on white, WCAG 3:1 already sits at about Lc 56 to 60, so the two models agree; on the dark surface #2B415F, WCAG 3:1 colours reach only Lc 31 to 37 and Lc 60 needs about 5.8:1. The dark theme is where the models disagree and where the current palette fails.
- Re-fitting Okabe-Ito in OKLCH at one lightness per theme is the right mechanism (at fixed L the WCAG ratio varies only 1.2x across hues) but it destroys what made Okabe-Ito colour-blind safe, which is lightness spacing: at one L, orange and yellow collapse to CIEDE2000 0.7 under deuteranopia and the two blues to 3.6 in normal vision. Two lightness bands above the contrast floor restore a worst-case pairwise CIEDE2000 of about 8 to 11; one band supports four hues robustly, not six.
- Thresholds: CIEDE2000 of 1 to 2 is barely distinguishable, 6.86 is the literature floor for "easily distinguishable", Palettailor enforces 10 (also against the background), CVD-simulated tools use 6 to 12. These are conventions; adopt 10 in normal vision and 8 after simulation as project rules and always add dash patterns and direct end labels.
- SC 2.5.7 is confirmed: the press-drag seek needs a single-pointer alternative (a tap on the track that commits, or step buttons); keyboard support alone does not count. SC 2.5.8 wants 24 x 24 CSS px targets for the handle and chapter marks, or 24 px spacing, and treats the slider itself as one target.
- ARIA 1.2 makes slider descendants presentational, so role=slider on the chart <svg> (the current pattern) hides every tick label and series from assistive technology. Put the slider role on the thumb group, graphics-document on the svg, graphics-object on each series, and ship a real HTML table.
- Announcements: the slider role already speaks aria-valuetext; do not add a live region for peeking or pending seeks; debounce clock-driven value text by about 300 ms but never keyboard changes; keep one pre-existing polite status region for commit and play/pause.
- Forced-colors mode preserves SVG author colours (UA stylesheet: svg { forced-color-adjust: preserve-parent-color }), so hard-coded series strokes can vanish on a forced canvas; add a forced-colors rule that uses system colours and dash patterns.
- Focus: draw the ring inside the SVG (stroke currentColor, 2 px, 3:1 change) rather than with CSS outline, which scales and clips in SVG user space; 2.4.13 gives the 2 CSS px perimeter target.
- Testing: build-time Rust via the palette crate (Oklch, Ciede2000, Wcag21RelativeContrast) and farg (Brettel, Machado, Vienot); real-render checks via CDP Emulation.setEmulatedVisionDeficiency (Machado matrices) and Emulation.setEmulatedMedia (forced-colors, prefers-color-scheme, prefers-reduced-motion), computed styles via CSS.getComputedStyleForNode, and the accessibility tree via Accessibility.getFullAXTree.

## Findings

Question 1: WCAG 2.2 requirements that bite charts

1. SC 1.4.11 Non-text Contrast (https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html). Normative text: "The visual presentation of the following have a contrast ratio of at least 3:1 against adjacent color(s): ... Graphical Objects: Parts of graphics required to understand the content, except when a particular presentation of graphics is essential to the information being conveyed." The Understanding document makes the chart case explicit:
   - "The term 'graphical object' applies to stand-alone icons such as a print icon (with no text), and the important parts of a more complex diagram such as each line in a graph."
   - Line-chart example (Figure 38): "In order to understand the graph you need to discern the lines and shapes for each condition. To perceive the values of each line along the chart you need to discern the grey lines marking the graduated 100 value increments. The graphical objects are the lines in the graph, including the background lines for the values, and the colored lines with shapes. The lines should have 3:1 contrast against their background, but as there is little overlap with other lines they do not need to contrast with each other or the graduated lines."
   - So: series against surface, yes; series against series, only where they overlap; gridlines against surface, yes when the reader needs them to read values.
   - "Required for understanding" exceptions: not required when "A graphic with text embedded or overlaid conveys the same information, such as labels and values on a chart" (with the note "Text within a graphic must meet 1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum)"), when the graphic is aesthetic, when "The information is available in another form, such as in a table that follows the graph", or for logos.
   - Pie-chart examples: Figure 44 fails because "the contrast between the slices is not 3:1 or greater"; Figure 45 is "Not applicable" because "visible labels and values ... convey equivalent information to the graphical objects"; Figure 46 passes with "A darker border ... around the yellow slice".
   - Thin lines: "Due to anti-aliasing, particularly thin lines and shapes of non-text elements may be rendered by user agents with a much fainter color than the actual color defined in the underlying CSS. This can lead to situations where non-text elements have a contrast ratio that nominally passes the Success Criterion, but have a much lower contrast in practice. In these cases, best practice would be for authors to avoid particularly thin lines and shapes, or to use a combination of colors that exceeds the normative requirements." Conformance is evaluated on the specified colours, not the anti-aliased pixels.
   - Thresholds are not rounded: "2.999:1 would not meet the 3:1 threshold."
   - Slider thumb: "For visual information required to identify a state, such as the check in a checkbox or the thumb of a slider, that part might be within the component so the adjacent color might be another part of the component."
   - Focus indicator: must contrast 3:1 with the adjacent colours (outside the component: with the background it sits on); the criterion "does not directly compare the focused and unfocused states" (that is 2.4.13).
   - Testing principles: identify each graphic "excluding graphics which have visible text for the same information, or are decorative"; "If there are multiple colors and/or a gradient, choose the least contrasting area to test"; "If the least-contrasting area is less than 3:1, assume that area is invisible, is the graphical object still understandable?"
   - Dynamic graphics: "the unfocused default version must already have sufficiently contrasting colors or text"; hover or focus may then foreground a series.
   - Hover treatments themselves do not need 3:1 (the pointer is the indicator) but "does not cause a component itself to lose sufficient contrast".
   Implication: the band, the marks' dashed rules and the playhead line are all graphical objects unless their information is also in text; the peek readout, direct labels and the table are the cheapest way to take the grid and the band out of scope.

2. SC 1.4.1 Use of Color (https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html). "Color is not used as the only visual means of conveying information, indicating an action, prompting a response, or distinguishing a visual element." Notes: "If content is conveyed through the use of colors that differ not only in their hue, but that also have a significant difference in lightness, then this counts as an additional visual distinction, as long as the difference in relative luminance between the colors leads to a contrast ratio of 3:1 or greater"; but "if content relies on the user's ability to accurately perceive or differentiate a particular color an additional visual indicator will be required regardless of the contrast ratio". Sufficient techniques for colour inside an image: G111 "Using color and pattern" and G14 "Ensuring that information conveyed by color differences is also available in text"; failure F13 covers text alternatives that omit colour-coded information. The document stresses that the criterion targets sighted users who cannot distinguish colours and is not met by exposing the information to assistive technology alone. Okabe and Ito (https://jfly.uni-koeln.de/color/) give the chart version: "label elements of the graph on the graph itself rather than making a separate color-coded key, since matching same colors in distant places is extremely difficult"; "Show difference BOTH in color and shape (solid and dotted lines, different symbols, various hatching, etc.)"; "Make texts and objects as thick or big as possible ... Colorblind people find it especially difficult to distinguish the colors of thin lines and small symbols"; "Even for non-colorblinds, thin lines and small characters in blue and yellow are hard read. For thin lines and small objects, use darker blue and orange"; and "avoid using red characters on black backgrounds" because for protanopes "dark red appears almost as black" (vermilion instead of pure red).

3. SC 1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum) and 1.4.6 (https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html). Text and images of text need 4.5:1; large-scale text (18 pt, or 14 pt bold; "14pt and 18pt are equivalent to approximately 18.5px and 24px") needs 3:1; 1.4.6 asks 7:1, justified by 20/80 acuity. The same anti-aliasing caveat applies to "particularly thin or unusual fonts". The document says charts are covered by 1.4.11, but text inside an SVG is text: tick labels, end labels, the playhead readout and chapter names are 1.4.3 items, measured against whatever they are drawn on (a label on the band is measured against the band). The rationale is spelled out (3:1 from ISO 9241-3 and ANSI HFES 100 for normal vision, times a 1.5 contrast-sensitivity loss at 20/40, hence 4.5:1), together with the advisory against "red on black" for protanopia.

4. SC 2.5.7 Dragging Movements (https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html). Normative: "All functionality that uses a dragging movement for operation can be achieved by a single pointer without dragging, unless dragging is essential or the functionality is determined by the user agent and not modified by the author." Understanding: "while a range slider is operated by dragging the slider thumb, an alternative pointer method to change the value is to click/tap anywhere on the slider track to move the thumb to that position"; "achieving keyboard equivalence for a dragging operation does not automatically meet this success criterion, unless that equivalent keyboard operation also provides controls that can be clicked or tapped with a pointer"; "providing a text input can be an acceptable single-pointer alternative to dragging"; the alternative "can't exclusively rely on a path-based gesture, such as swiping or 'flicking'" (2.5.1); the exemption for scrolling does not apply when "content actively suppresses the user agent's own scrolling functionality and/or implements its own scrolling mechanism", which is our case since the film interprets the pointer. A native input type=range would pass under the user-agent exception, which is one argument for keeping a hidden native range as a fallback control.

5. SC 2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum) (https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html). "The size of the target for pointer inputs is at least 24 by 24 CSS pixels" with five exceptions: spacing ("if a 24 CSS pixel diameter circle is centered on the bounding box of each, the circles do not intersect another target or the circle for another undersized target"), equivalent control on the same page, inline text, user-agent control, essential. Note 1: "Targets that allow for values to be selected spatially based on position within the target are considered one target for the purpose of the success criterion. Examples include sliders." The essential exception names "an interactive data visualization where targets are necessarily dense" and adds "authors are strongly encouraged to provide equivalent functionality through alternative means to the extent practical." The size test: "it must be conceptually possible to draw a solid 24 by 24 CSS pixel square, aligned to the horizontal and vertical axis such that the square is completely within the target"; zoom does not help ("authors cannot meet it by claiming that the target will have enough spacing or sufficient size if the user zooms"); for "a more complex clickable SVG shape" the circle is centred on the bounding box. For us the track is one target and passes; the playhead handle, chapter marks and any focusable points are separate targets that need 24 px hit areas or spacing; hover peeking is not a target.

6. SC 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (Minimum) (https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-not-obscured-minimum.html) and SC 2.4.13 Focus Appearance (https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-appearance.html). 2.4.11 (AA): "When a user interface component receives keyboard focus, the component is not entirely hidden due to author-created content"; typical offenders are "sticky footers, sticky headers, and non-modal dialogs"; "semi-transparent overlays, or overlays that apply a blur or other dimming effect" that partly cover the focused item may pass 2.4.11 yet fail 1.4.11 for the focus indicator; the criterion evaluates the component, not its indicator. 2.4.13 (AAA) is the concrete design target: an indicator area "at least as large as the area of a 2 CSS pixel thick perimeter of the unfocused component" with "a contrast ratio of at least 3:1 between the same pixels in the focused and unfocused states"; area formulas 4h + 4w for rectangles and 4 pi r for circles; a 2 px outline, outset or border passes, a 2 px inset ring does not ("it would need to be at least 3px thick"); "Pixels modified by user agent resolution enhancements and anti-aliasing can be ignored." For a playhead that travels while playing: sticky page chrome must never cover the thumb at either end of the track, and the ring must be drawn where the thumb is now.

7. SC 2.3.3 Animation from Interactions (AAA) (https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html). "Motion animation triggered by interaction can be disabled, unless the animation is essential to the functionality or the information being conveyed." Sufficient techniques: C39 (the CSS prefers-reduced-motion query), SCR40 (the same query from script), or a site-wide preference. Motion the page starts by itself (a playing film) is instead 2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide, which the film's pause covers. MDN's reference (https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/prefers-reduced-motion) lists the OS settings that set the preference and notes that "@media (prefers-reduced-motion)" alone is equivalent to "reduce". For the chart: the playhead's position change is essential (it is the information); the eased travel, the thumb blend and the travelling ghost are not.

Question 1, continued: WCAG 3 and APCA on thin lines

8. WCAG 3.0 Working Draft, 3 March 2026 (https://www.w3.org/TR/wcag-3.0/). The draft "includes all requirements that have reached the developing status" and says of itself "it still has several years of work. Details will change". The relevant requirements, all at Developing status: "Graphical object contrast sufficient: Parts of graphical object required to understand the content meet a minimum contrast ratio test", except when "a particular presentation of graphical objects is essential to the information being conveyed"; "Interactive element contrast sufficient: Parts of interactive elements required to understand their function meet a minimum contrast ratio test"; "Focus indicator contrast sufficient: If a custom focus indicator is used, it has sufficient adjacent contrast and change of contrast"; and "Non-text content not relied on". The phrase "contrast ratio test" is a dangling definition: no algorithm (WCAG 2 ratio or APCA) and no numeric threshold appear anywhere in the draft. The WebKit contrast-color() post (https://webkit.org/blog/16929/contrast-color/) describes the same state from the CSS side: the CSS standard "still calls for browsers to use the older algorithm", the spec note says WCAG 2.1 "is known to have problems, particularly on dark backgrounds", and "Debates over which algorithm is best for WCAG 3 are still ongoing, including discussion of licensing of the algorithms under consideration." Conclusion: nothing in WCAG 3 is normative today; WCAG 2.2 is the conformance target and APCA is design guidance.

9. APCA guidance and where its stroke-width numbers come from. The APCA documentation by Andrew Somers (https://git.apcacontrast.com/documentation/APCA_in_a_Nutshell) defines Lc levels: Lc 90 preferred for body text; Lc 75 minimum for body text; Lc 60 minimum for other content text; Lc 45 minimum for large headlines and "pictograms with fine details, or smaller outline icons"; Lc 30 "the minimum for large/solid semantic & understandable non-text elements such as 'mostly solid' icons or pictograms. Generally no less than 5.5px solid in its smallest dimension"; Lc 15 "the absolute minimum for any non-semantic non-text that needs to be discernible, and is no less than 5px (solid) in its smallest dimension. This may include dividers ... but does not include fine details which have a higher minimum. Designers should treat anything below this level as invisible." The companion page (https://git.apcacontrast.com/documentation/WhyAPCA) argues that "WCAG 2.x contrast cannot be used for guidance designing 'dark mode'" and that "No single figure such as 4.5:1 or 3:1 can be used as a blanket target for contrast between two colors, without considering the use case, size, thickness, etc." The stroke-width figures come from a "Non-Text Minimums" table Somers posted marked "Preliminary, Feb 2 2022"; the Dataviz Contrast tool by Jon Olav Eikenes (http://datavizcontrast.com/) states that its requirements "are extracted from the Non-Text Minimums table (image) from Myndex" and reproduces them: below 1 px Lc 100, 1 px Lc 90, 1.5 px Lc 75, 2 px Lc 60, 3 px Lc 50, 4 px Lc 45, 6 px Lc 30, 8 px Lc 25, 10 px Lc 20, 15 px Lc 15. The SAPC-APCA discussion #117 (https://github.com/Myndex/SAPC-APCA/discussions/117, April 2024 to March 2025) holds the caveats and the dissent:
   - Eikenes, building a dataviz design system, reads the table as "lines of 2px width in a line chart need 60 Lc, while larger areas such as bars in a bar chart might do with 30 Lc", and objects that if 1.5 px gridlines must reach Lc 60 the separation between the grid and a 3 px data line falls to "only 8 Lc", quoting Tufte: "Dark grid lines are chartjunk." He also shows an IPCC chart whose layered elements sit at Lc 17 to 47 and asks whether alternatives (table, audio, text) can stand in for contrast in layered graphics.
   - Somers: "The problem with 1px lines is, depending on how they are rasterized to the screen, can lose as much as Lc 30 in contrast, due to blending/antialiasing effects. This is less a problem on retina displays, but is certainly an issue on 72ppi displays"; gridlines are "container" or "assistive" elements, "The semantic is the symbols/numbers/text on the axis. The 'understanding-the-content' element is the chart's blue line", and "If the element can be removed with no appreciable effect on the understanding ... then it is an ancillary element"; the 1.5 px gridline at Lc 60 "came out of the lab, but is not the end all, and still part of the active investigation"; "Bridge PCA Lc 60 is backwards compatible with WCAG 2's 3:1"; and an interim rule for thickness in CSS px, "lineThickness = Math.pow(LcValue/100, -1.5)" (Lc 60 gives 2.2 px, Lc 75 gives 1.5 px, Lc 90 gives 1.2 px), "with different exponents depending on the use case".
   Honest position: APCA's non-text numbers are one researcher's preliminary lab values, contested for gridlines even by sympathetic users, and not adopted by W3C; but they are the only quantitative guidance on stroke width found, and the WCAG Understanding text independently says thin lines need more than the nominal ratio.

10. What the two models say about our surfaces (computed here). For the seven Okabe-Ito hues re-fitted at chroma 0.14, with WCAG luminance from the Understanding key terms and APCA constants from the perceive-color port (https://docs.rs/crate/perceive-color/latest/source/src/apca.rs):
   - On white: WCAG 3.0:1 corresponds to about Lc 56 to 60 (L 0.65 to 0.68 depending on hue) and WCAG 4.5:1 to about Lc 72 to 76 (L 0.55 to 0.58). Lc 60 needs about 3.3:1, Lc 75 about 5.2:1. On light surfaces the models nearly coincide: a 2 px line that clears 3:1 with a little margin also clears APCA's 2 px figure.
   - On #2B415F: WCAG 3.0:1 corresponds to only Lc 31 to 37 (L 0.62 to 0.65). Lc 45 needs about 4.3:1 (L 0.71 to 0.75), Lc 60 about 5.8:1 (L 0.80 to 0.83), Lc 75 about 7.5:1 (L 0.87 to 0.90). This is the "dark mode" disagreement in concrete form: by APCA's table, series that merely pass WCAG 3:1 on our dark surface would need 5 to 6 px strokes; at 2 px they need roughly 5.8:1.
   - The brief's measurements reproduce exactly (#0072B2 2.00:1 and #D55E00 2.68:1 on #2B415F, #E69F00 2.25:1 on white); the untouched Okabe-Ito yellow #F0E442 gives 1.32:1 on white; sky blue #56B4E9 gives 2.31:1 on white and 4.50:1 on #2B415F.

Question 2: palettes, re-fitting and thresholds

11. Reference palettes.
   - Okabe-Ito (https://jfly.uni-koeln.de/color/): a palette "unambiguous both to colorblinds and non-colorblinds", "with vivid colors so that color names are easy to identify", printable; "For red, vermilion is used since it is recognizable also to protanopes. Colors between yellow and green are all avoided ... For green, bluish green is chosen so that it won't be confused with red or brown ... reddish purple is chosen. Between vermilion and yellow, three colors with different apparent intensity is selected. Sky blue and blue are chosen so that they are distinguishable with there difference in brightness and saturation." That last sentence is the crux for re-fitting: several pairs are separated by lightness, not hue. Computed here, the OKLCH hue angles are sky blue 236 and blue 244 (8 degrees apart), vermilion 48, orange 77 and yellow 105 (all within 60 degrees), bluish green 166 and reddish purple 346. Their combination rule: "Use 'warm' and 'cool' colors alternatively. When using two warm colors or two cool colors, put distinct differences in brightness or saturation. Avoid combination of colors with low saturation or low brightness."
   - Paul Tol (https://sronpersonalpages.nl/~pault/, updated August 2026): schemes designed "with the help of mathematical descriptions of colour differences and the two main types of colour-blind vision" to be "distinct for all people, including colour-blind readers; distinct from black and white; distinct on screen and paper; matching well together". Bright scheme (#4477AA, #EE6677, #228833, #CCBB44, #66CCEE, #AA3377, #BBBBBB) is the default for lines. "If there is a requirement that the contrast ratio of the colours with white ... is at least 4.5, the blue, green and purple from the bright scheme can be used, or any of the eleven colours from the ... dark scheme ... but the colours in the dark scheme are less distinct from each other" and "The differences are too small for a monochrome printout"; the three-colour high-contrast scheme (#004488, #DDAA33, #BB5566) "also works well for people with monochrome vision and in a monochrome printout"; the medium-contrast scheme is built as three light/dark pairs. This is the clearest published statement of the contrast-versus-distinctness trade-off and it matches the computed results below.
   - IBM Carbon (https://carbondesignsystem.com/data-visualization/color-palettes/): a 14-step categorical sequence with separate light and dark variants, "carefully curated to maximize contrast between neighboring colors"; the light sequence begins Purple 70 #6929c4, Cyan 50 #1192e8, Teal 70 #005d5d, Magenta 70 #9f1853, Red 50 #fa4d56, Red 90 #570408, Green 60 #198038, Blue 80 #002d9c; single-colour charts use Purple 70, Blue 80, Cyan 50 or Teal 70; for sequential ramps "In light themes, the darkest color denotes the largest values. In dark themes, the lightest color denotes the largest values."
   - Tableau 10 (Maureen Stone, https://www.tableau.com/blog/colors-upgrade-tableau-10-56782): redesigned in a CIELAB tool that previews "our hardest-to-distinguish marks (scatterplot default)", stacked adjacency and text readability; the new palette is "softer, more sophisticated, less 'Crayola bright'"; Tableau 20 is "light-dark pairs of these basic colors"; sequential defaults moved to blue and orange-blue because they are "much more friendly to our users with common forms of color-vision deficiencies"; small colour-picker swatches needed added chroma because "a large area of any color looks brighter and more colorful than a small one" (the same effect applies to 2 px lines versus filled bands).
   - Viridis (https://bids.github.io/colormap/): designed so that "it will analytically be perfectly perceptually-uniform, both in regular form and also when converted to black-and-white", in CAM02-UCS, using "the same general bluish to reddish to yellowish sequence, because this is relatively friendly to common forms of colorblindness"; the viscm tool shows moderate deuteranomaly, deuteranopia, protanopia and tritanopia simulations. It is the right basis for any sequential encoding (a density strip, a heat band), not for categorical series.

12. Minimum perceptual distance between series (CIEDE2000).
   - Di Bartolomeo, Buchmüller, Frings, Fuchs and Keim, IVAPP 2025 (https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/bitstreams/9bb069fc-62b3-4c5c-ac80-5a04c3e472b8/download): "A difference between colors of 1 or 2 is just barely distinguishable, and certainly not viable to distinguish different categories in a visualization. According to Green-Armytage (Green-Armytage, 2006), the minimal pairwise distance for colors to be easily distinguishable is 6.86. However, we can err on the safe side and aim for a minimum larger distance." Their Table 1 minimum pairwise CIEDE2000 for the d3 defaults: Tableau10 18.1, Category10 16.2, Observable10 18, Set1 15.4, Set2 16.8, Set3 9.5, Pastel1 6.8, Pastel2 8.7, Paired 13.8, Accent 21.6, Dark2 17.4; and their takeaway that "The maximum distance between colors is not always the best option, and reducing the distance by a minimal amount might improve the aesthetic appeal of the palette."
   - Palettailor (Lu et al., IEEE TVCG 2020, https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2009.02969): "we employ a hard constraint into our optimization: each two colors should have a minimal CIEDE2000 distance of 10, so that each pair of colors can be distinguished even for large spatial distances ... In addition, we also take the background color c0 into consideration to prevent the optimization from generating colors indistinguishable from the background."
   - CatPAL (arXiv 2608.09108, 2026, https://arxiv.org/html/2608.09108): greedy palette construction "filters by a minimum perceptual distance (delta E >= 12) from existing palette members" (CIELAB delta E).
   - After CVD simulation the community uses lower floors. palettecore (https://pypi.org/project/palettecore/0.2.1/) computes pairwise CIEDE2000 "under simulated protanopia, deuteranopia, tritanopia" with defaults "normal >= 8, each CVD condition >= 6" and states they "are package-default design rules, not established universal accessibility cut-offs". PaletteGuard (https://github.com/Rudevin17/PaletteGuard) simulates the three dichromacies and derives its pass floors from the Okabe-Ito (Wong) palette's own worst pairs: protanopia 13.38, deuteranopia 12.19, tritanopia 9.79, failing at 0.75 of those; its audit shows d3 category10 breaking at three series (orange versus green, delta E 4.69 under protanopia) and Tableau10 reaching 1.12 under protanopia.
   - Computed here with the Machado 2009 model at severity 1.0: the original Okabe-Ito seven have minimum pairwise CIEDE2000 21.7 in normal vision, 12.2 under protanopia, 11.6 under deuteranopia and 10.9 under tritanopia, in line with PaletteGuard's figures from a different model.
   Reading: 10 in normal vision (Palettailor) and about 8 to 10 under dichromat simulation (what Okabe-Ito itself achieves) are defensible project floors; below 7 is documented as hard to tell apart; none of it is a standard, and the number depends on the simulation model.

13. Re-fitting hues in OKLCH: mechanism, evidence, and what it does to Okabe-Ito.
   - Mechanism: MDN (https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/color_value/oklch) defines oklch() L as "the color's perceived lightness" (0 to 1 or 0% to 100%), C as chroma where "100% is equal to 0.4" and practical values "in practice does not exceed 0.5", H in degrees, and the relative syntax "oklch(from var(--color) l c h / calc(alpha - 0.1))" with calc() on any channel. color-mix() (https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/color_value/color-mix) interpolates in oklab by default, in oklch "if avoiding graying out in color mixing is desired", with shorter or longer hue arcs. light-dark() (https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/color_value/light-dark) "returns the first value if the used color scheme is light or if no preference is set and the second value if the used color scheme is dark" and requires "color-scheme: light dark" on :root. CSS Color 5 (https://drafts.csswg.org/css-color-5/) classes light-dark(), contrast-color() and system colours as non-absolute colours, which matters when a test reads computed values.
   - Evidence that one L fixes contrast for a whole hue row: the richdevtools measurement (https://richdevtools.com/articles/web/oklch-vs-hsl-shade-scales, August 2026) sweeps hue at OKLCH L 0.62, C 0.14 and finds WCAG contrast on white between 3.28 (hue 180) and 3.92 (hue 0), "a 1.2x spread versus 12.9x for HSL" at HSL L 50%; the recipe is "pick the lightness ramp first, then find the maximum chroma that stays in gamut across every hue in your set, and use that as the ceiling" and "Check contrast at the step, not at the colour."
   - Computed here on our surfaces at chroma cap 0.14: the L at which each Okabe-Ito hue reaches 3.0:1 on white lies between 0.65 (bluish green, binding) and 0.69 (reddish purple); for 4.5:1 between 0.55 and 0.59. On #2B415F the L for 3.0:1 lies between 0.62 (bluish green) and 0.65 (reddish purple, binding); for 4.5:1 between 0.72 and 0.76. So one L per theme does set the ratio for all hues within a few percent, as claimed.
   - What that costs: at a single L the palette loses the lightness separation Okabe-Ito relies on. Computed here: six hues at L 0.55 on white with both blues kept give minimum pairwise CIEDE2000 3.6 in normal vision and 1.0 to 1.5 under CVD (sky blue versus blue); dropping sky blue and keeping the other six at one L gives 13.6 to 15.7 in normal vision but 0.7 to 3.0 under protanopia and deuteranopia, because orange, yellow and vermilion become one colour. Yellow at any L that clears 3:1 on white is an olive (#9C9300); this is the hue Datawrapper warns "will turn into a brown".
   - What works: two lightness bands, both above the contrast floor. Exhaustive search over the seven hues, six chosen, two bands, maximising the worst-case minimum distance across normal vision and the three dichromacies (computed here):
     Light theme, bands L 0.55 and 0.65, chroma cap 0.16, surface white: orange #976700 (4.94:1, Lc 74), sky blue #007BAD (4.76:1, Lc 72) and reddish purple #B04285 (5.30:1, Lc 76) in the darker band; bluish green #00A97B (3.03:1, Lc 56), yellow as olive #9C9300 (3.19:1, Lc 59) and blue #0196E8 (3.20:1, Lc 58) in the lighter band. Minimum pairwise CIEDE2000: 11.7 normal, 10.9 protanopia, 11.1 deuteranopia, 8.5 tritanopia (bluish green versus blue).
     Dark theme, bands L 0.66 and 0.76, cap 0.16, surface #2B415F: orange #C18500 (3.27:1, Lc 33), bluish green #00AC7E (3.56:1, Lc 37), blue #0D9AEC (3.37:1, Lc 35) and reddish purple #D564A6 (3.07:1, Lc 31) in the lower band; sky blue #40BEFF (4.94:1, Lc 52) and yellow #C1B508 (4.90:1, Lc 51) in the upper band. Distances 10.9, 9.4, 9.2, 8.5. The Lc column shows the problem: the lower band passes WCAG and misses APCA's 2 px figure by a wide margin.
     Dark theme with an APCA floor instead, bands L 0.75 and 0.85 (Lc >= 45, about 4.3:1 or better): orange #DFA133, sky blue #40BAFA, reddish purple #ED87C0 (Lc 46 to 50); bluish green #63EBB8, yellow #DBD35C, blue #A1D4FF (Lc 67 to 70). Distances 10.7 normal, 8.6 protanopia, 8.1 deuteranopia, 7.9 tritanopia. With an Lc 60 floor (bands 0.83 and 0.92) the colours become pastels and the CVD minimum falls to 6.6.
     One band supports four hues robustly: orange, bluish green, blue and reddish purple at L 0.65 on white (0.66 on the dark surface) give 37.5 normal, 12.1 protanopia, 15.9 deuteranopia, 7.9 tritanopia (8.5 at cap 0.16); adding a fifth hue in the same band drops the deuteranopia minimum to 4.2 to 5.5 (orange versus vermilion).
   - Datawrapper's production experience agrees (https://www.datawrapper.de/blog/colors-for-data-vis-style-guides): automatic dark mode "adjust[s] the lightness of your colors so that the contrast between the background and your chart elements is the same in both dark and light mode", but "A vibrant dark blue will convert into such a desaturated color that, although very bright on a dark background, it won't get much attention from readers" and "An exciting bright yellow on a bright background will turn into a brown on a dark background"; the options are "Create an alternative palette for dark mode" or choose hues "like green and pink that look fairly saturated when either dark or bright". Their 2024 guide (https://www.datawrapper.de/blog/create-good-color-palettes) recommends the same order of operations: "Define lightness and saturation first, then pick the hue", using an OKLCH picker, with a different lightness level per colour ("40, 48, 56, 64, 72, and 80%") so the palette survives greyscale.
   - contrast-color(): Baseline Newly Available since 2026-04-10, in Chrome 147, Firefox 146 and Safari 26 (https://web-platform-dx.github.io/web-features-explorer/features/contrast-color/); per the WebKit post it "simply chooses black or white to contrast with the first color" using the WCAG 2 algorithm, with candidate lists and target ratios deferred so the algorithm can be swapped later. Useful for label backplates or the readout on the playhead, not for series colours.

14. Simulating colour-vision deficiency and user preferences in CI.
   - Chrome's implementation (Mathias Bynens, https://developer.chrome.com/blog/cvd/) renders vision deficiencies as a colour-matrix filter in the Blink renderer; the matrices "are based on a physiologically accurate color vision deficiency simulation model by Machado, Oliveira, and Fernandes"; the same feature drives the DevTools Rendering tab and Puppeteer's page.emulateVisionDeficiency(type) (https://pptr.dev/api/puppeteer.page.emulatevisiondeficiency), whose example loops achromatopsia, deuteranopia, blurredVision and reducedContrast into screenshots.
   - CDP Emulation.setEmulatedVisionDeficiency (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ChromeDevTools/devtools-protocol/master/pdl/domains/Emulation.pdl): one parameter, type, enum none, blurredVision, reducedContrast, achromatopsia, deuteranopia, protanopia, tritanopia, with the comment "best-effort emulations come first, followed by any physiologically accurate emulations for medically recognized color vision deficiencies". Emulation.setEmulatedMedia takes an optional media string and an optional features array of MediaFeature name/value pairs. The Rendering tab documentation (https://developer.chrome.com/docs/devtools/rendering/emulate-css) lists what Chrome emulates: prefers-color-scheme (light, dark), forced-colors (active, none), prefers-contrast (more, less, custom), prefers-reduced-motion (reduce), prefers-reduced-transparency (reduce), color-gamut (srgb, p3, rec2020). Puppeteer's page.emulateMediaFeatures (https://pptr.dev/api/puppeteer.page.emulatemediafeatures) demonstrates prefers-color-scheme, prefers-reduced-motion and color-gamut through matchMedia; Playwright's page.emulateMedia (https://playwright.dev/docs/api/class-page) exposes colorScheme (light, dark), reducedMotion (reduce, no-preference), forcedColors (active, none) and contrast (more, no-preference). The CSS Color Adjust CR (https://drafts.csswg.org/css-color-adjust-1/) now standardises forced-colors emulation "For the purposes of user agent automation and application testing" with a ForcedColorsModeAutomationTheme of none, light or dark and defined system-colour mappings for light and dark.
   - Reading rendered values: CSS.getComputedStyleForNode "Returns the computed style for a DOM node identified by nodeId" as an array of name/value pairs, so stroke, stroke-width, stroke-dasharray, fill and opacity can be read for every series node; Accessibility.getFullAXTree "Fetches the entire accessibility tree for the root Document" (optional depth and frameId) returning AXNode objects with role, chromeRole, name, description, value, properties, ignored and ignoredReasons; Accessibility.getPartialAXTree does the same for one node with fetchRelatives (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ChromeDevTools/devtools-protocol/master/pdl/domains/CSS.pdl and https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ChromeDevTools/devtools-protocol/master/pdl/domains/Accessibility.pdl).
   - Offline models: DaltonLens-Python (https://github.com/DaltonLens/DaltonLens-Python) implements "the Viénot 1999, Brettel 1997 or Machado 2009 models" with a severity between 0 and 1 and a CLI; its companion article (https://daltonlens.org/understanding-cvd-simulation/) explains that all three go through linear RGB and LMS with one or two 3x3 matrices, that Brettel projects onto two half-planes, that Viénot is a single-matrix simplification for protan and deutan, and that Machado adds an opponent-colour stage and covers anomalous trichromacy. Chrome uses Machado; farg defaults to Brettel for dichromacy and Machado for anomalies. The numbers differ at the margins between models, which is one reason to carry a few units of slack in any threshold.
   - Rust: the palette crate's color_difference module (https://docs.rs/palette/latest/palette/color_difference/index.html) provides Ciede2000 ("The de-facto standard"), ImprovedCiede2000, DeltaE, EuclideanDistance, HyAb and Wcag21RelativeContrast ("Calculate and check the WCAG 2.1 relative contrast and relative luminance ... Mostly useful as a hint or for checking WCAG 2.1 compliance, considering the criticism it has received"), and the Oklch type (https://docs.rs/palette/latest/palette/struct.Oklch.html) with l, chroma and hue fields converting to and from Oklab and sRGB. farg's color_vision_deficiency module (https://docs.rs/farg/latest/farg/color_vision_deficiency/index.html) has brettel, machado and vienot submodules behind cvd-brettel, cvd-machado and cvd-vienot features; its usage guide (https://github.com/aaronmallen/farg/blob/main/docs/usage/cvd.md) shows brettel::protanopia(color.to_xyz()), machado::deuteranomaly(xyz, 0.5) and convenience methods simulate_protanopia() and simulate_deuteranomaly(severity) on Rgb<Srgb>. perceive-cvd (https://crates.io/crates/perceive-cvd) offers Brettel with severity and a Viénot fast path, no_std and wasm-ready. APCA ports: perceive-color (https://docs.rs/crate/perceive-color/latest/source/src/apca.rs) implements APCA-W3 0.0.98G-4g with a font lookup; apca-w3 (https://crates.io/crates/apca-w3) and fenestra-core (https://docs.rs/crate/fenestra-core/latest/source/src/apca.rs) carry the same constant set. None is the reference implementation, so pin the version and cross-check a known pair (black on white about Lc 106).
   - Caveat from the palette's own authors (Okabe and Ito): "not all colorblind people see colors as indicated in the colorblind proof ... In the proof, a range of colors that are difficult for colorblinds to distinguish are converted into one color ... the proof should only be used for identifying 'which colors on the document are difficult for them to judge.' In this respect, colorblind proof is not the true simulation of colorblind vision." Simulations find confusable pairs; they do not prove a palette safe, and they cannot replace testing with colour-blind readers.

15. Forced colours and SVG.
   - CSS Color Adjust Module Level 1, CR 7 May 2026 (https://drafts.csswg.org/css-color-adjust-1/): in forced colors mode "the components of all properties on the element are force-adjusted to the user's preferred color palette"; background-color is forced to the opposite of the forced text colour with its alpha preserved; box-shadow and text-shadow compute to none; background-image to none unless url(); and "if its computed value is a color other than a system color, its used value is instead forced to a system color", so author-specified system colours survive. Crucially: "In order to not break SVG content, UAs are expected to add the following rules to their UA style sheet: svg|svg { forced-color-adjust: preserve-parent-color; } svg|foreignObject { forced-color-adjust: auto; }", where preserve-parent-color means "if the color property inherits from its parent (i.e. there is no cascaded value or the cascaded value is currentColor, inherit, or another keyword that inherits from the parent), then it computes to the used color of its parent's color value. In all other respects, behaves the same as none"; the note says the value exists "to get a reasonable behavior from embedded SVG elements that expect to receive the outer document's text color ... while otherwise defaulting SVGs to preserving their exact colors, as forced colors mode can't generally be usefully applied to illustrations."
   - The Edge team's guidance (https://blogs.windows.com/msedgedev/2020/09/17/styling-for-windows-high-contrast-with-new-standards-for-forced-colors/): "Forced color modes do not adjust SVGs: these can be very complex documents, and the web developer has better insights than the platform as to how an SVG should be adjusted or not"; use currentColor or the system colours (CanvasText, Canvas, LinkText, GrayText, HighlightText and Highlight, ButtonText and ButtonFace); "web developers generally should avoid using static colors in forced color modes"; box-shadow is reverted, so state carried by shadows disappears (use a transparent outline that the mode will colour); "In Chromium, a forced background with a luminosity of < 0.33 will match prefers-color-scheme: dark"; "the use of CSS system colors by an author will override those set by the user agent" without forced-color-adjust: none. Melanie Richards (https://melanie-richards.com/blog/currentcolor-svg-hcm/) documents that moving forcing to used-value time broke the currentColor technique in Chromium until preserve-parent-color landed (Polypane places the regression in Chromium 89 to 102, https://polypane.app/blog/forced-colors-explained-a-practical-guide/); her interim advice was explicit system colours on SVG parts, which remains the belt-and-braces option.
   - MDN (https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/forced-colors and https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/forced-color-adjust): forced-colors: active is set only when the OS mode is on; browsers draw "backplates" behind text; forced-color-adjust: none disables adjustment and backplates and "should not be used to prevent user choices being respected"; the forced system colour "depend[s] on the context of the element" and "based on native element semantics, not on added ARIA roles".
   - The APG media seek slider (https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/slider/examples/slider-seek/) is the worked example for our widget: "the color of the focus ring border is set to match the foreground color of high contrast mode text by specifying the CSS currentcolor value for the stroke property of the inline SVG rect element used to draw the focus ring border. To make the background of the rect match the high contrast background color, the fill-opacity attribute of the rect is set to zero. If specific colors were instead used ... those colors would remain the same in high contrast mode, which could lead to insufficient contrast between the rail and the thumb or even make them invisible ... Note: The SVG element needs to have the CSS forced-color-adjust property set to auto for the currentcolor value to be updated in high contrast mode."
   Implication: series strokes, band fill and playhead colour will be kept verbatim on a user's forced Canvas, which may be black, white or any colour; without an explicit rule the chart can be invisible, and the only reliable identities left are CanvasText, LinkText, Highlight and GrayText plus dash patterns and labels.

Question 3: keyboard and screen-reader access

16. Roles. WAI-ARIA Graphics Module 1.0 (Recommendation, https://www.w3.org/TR/graphics-aria-1.0/) defines three roles. graphics-document: "A type of document in which the visual appearance or layout of content conveys meaning", superclass document, accessible name required, intended for "charts, maps, diagrams, and technical drawings", nestable so that "navigation between inner and outer graphics remains explicit". graphics-object: "A section of a graphics-document that represents a distinct object or sub-component with semantic meaning. A graphical object may itself have nested sub-components", superclass group, name from author or contents (not required), "Avoid assigning roles to grouping elements serving only styling or layout purposes", and user agents "should support hierarchical navigation of nested objects similar to nested lists". graphics-symbol: "A graphical object used to convey a simple meaning or category, where the meaning is more important than the particular visual appearance", superclass img, name required, children presentational, with aria-roledescription to name symbol types. The platform mappings are still drafts: the SVG-AAM Working Draft of 5 August 2026 (https://www.w3.org/TR/svg-aam-1.0/) says "This is an early draft that contains outdated information and errors. Please do not implement based on this draft", while restating the model we rely on: SVG 2 keyboard navigation follows the HTML tabindex model; elements with tabindex or focus "MUST be included in the accessibility tree, without exception"; elements hidden by visibility and pointer-events are excluded unless aria-hidden is set to false.

17. The slider trap and the APG seek pattern. ARIA 1.2 (https://www.w3.org/TR/wai-aria-1.2/) lists slider with "Children Presentational: True", required aria-valuenow, aria-valuemin and aria-valuemax, supported aria-orientation, aria-readonly and aria-valuetext, and name required from author. The definition of children presentational: "The DOM descendants are presentational. User agents should not expose descendants of this element through the platform accessibility API. If user agents do not hide the descendant nodes, some information may be read twice." img has the same flag; group does not. Consequence: the film's role=slider on the chart <svg> makes tick labels, series, marks and chapter names invisible to assistive technology whatever roles they carry. The APG Slider Pattern (https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/slider/) puts focus "on the slider (the visual object that the mouse user would move, also known as the thumb)", requires the value triple, aria-valuetext when "the value of aria-valuenow is not user-friendly", aria-labelledby or aria-label, and Right/Up, Left/Down, Home, End with optional Page Up/Down; it warns that "Some users of touch-based assistive technologies may experience difficulty ... because the gestures their assistive technology provides for operating sliders may not yet generate the necessary output" and that authors "should fully test slider widgets using assistive technologies on devices where touch is a primary input mechanism". The seek example applies this to an SVG: role="none" on the svg "ensures assistive technologies do not interpret the SVG element as an image or some other role"; role="slider" and tabindex="0" on the thumb g "because it is the operable element that represents the slider value"; aria-valuetext gives the position in minutes and seconds ("if the play position were 4 minutes and 3 seconds from the start, the slider value is 243. If aria-valuetext were not used, assistive technology users would be told that the position is 243"); the maximum is included only at initialisation and on a focus change, "since repeating the maximum value every time the slider value changes is potentially distracting" (the page says "loses keyboard focus" in one place and "receives keyboard focus" in another; either way, not on every change); aria-hidden="true" on the rail rect "to prevent assistive technologies from presenting it as an image separate from the slider"; "The display of the slider's current value remains adjacent to the thumb as the thumb is moved, so people with a small field of view (e.g., due to magnification) can easily see the value"; Page Up/Down move 15 steps. MDN's aria-valuetext page (https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Accessibility/ARIA/Reference/Attributes/aria-valuetext) adds that "When both the aria-valuetext and aria-valuenow are included, the aria-valuetext is announced", and that both Element.ariaValueText and ElementInternals.ariaValueText reflect it, so a Rust custom element can set it through internals without attributes.

18. Structure beyond the slider: what W3C, libraries and research recommend.
   - W3C WAI complex-images tutorial (https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/images/complex/): give charts a short alt plus a long description that includes "scales, values, relationships and trends", ideally visible ("Make long descriptions available to everyone to reach a wider audience"), associated by figure/figcaption or a link; and the warning that "The element referenced by aria-describedby is treated as one continuous paragraph of text. Screen readers and other assistive technology do not have access to structural information, such as any headings and tables", so a table alternative must be real DOM.
   - Léonie Watson's ARIA table inside SVG (https://tink.uk/accessible-svg-line-graphs/): role=table, row, columnheader, rowheader and cell on g and text elements with title children; in 2017 it "works with Jaws in Chrome and IE, but only partially in Firefox ... NVDA struggles with the ARIA table semantics in Chrome, Firefox, and IE; whilst this solution works well with VoiceOver in Safari", so "it's a good idea to provide an alternative view of the information, for example by providing both a graphical and a tabular view". Nothing fetched this year shows that support became uniform; the technique is fragile.
   - Highcharts' accessibility module (https://www.highcharts.com/docs/accessibility/accessibility-module, https://www.highcharts.com/docs/accessibility/accessibility-module-feature-overview and https://www.highcharts.com/docs/accessibility/configure-the-accessibility-module): a hidden "information region" before the chart whose default format is a heading, "{typeDescription} {chartSubtitle} {chartLongdesc} {playAsSoundButton} {viewTableButton} {xAxisDescription} {yAxisDescription} {annotationsTitle}{annotationsList}", and an end-of-chart marker after; "the data series are marked up as regions/landmarks, which aids navigation between series"; "for charts with a large set of data points, individual data points are not by default exposed to screen reader users" (accessibility.series.pointDescriptionEnabledThreshold), and series.accessibility.exposeAsGroupOnly exposes a series as one item; keyboard navigation (https://github.com/highcharts/highcharts/blob/v13.0.1/ts/Accessibility/Options/A11yDefaults.ts) moves "left/right arrow keys ... between points in a series, while up/down arrow keys move between series" in "normal" mode or as one list in "serialize" mode, with wrapAround and a configurable order of series, zoom, range selector, navigator, legend and menu; the recommended description is visible text linked by linkedDescription, and hidden accessibility.description is discouraged because "Hidden descriptions are also prone to be forgotten if the chart is updated". The sonification module (https://www.highcharts.com/docs/sonification/getting-started) has instrument and speech tracks, "context tracks" for min, max, zero and rhythm, and scrubbing through playSegment.
   - Observable Plot (https://observablehq.com/plot/features/accessibility): ariaLabel and ariaDescription on the root svg; every mark g gets an aria-label with its type ("dot", "y-axis tick", "x-grid"); per-instance ariaLabel and ariaDescription channels for "a short, human-readable textual representation of each displayed data point"; ariaHidden "for decorative or redundant marks (such as rules or lines between dots)". Vega-Lite (https://vega.github.io/vega-lite/docs/mark.html) has aria (false sets aria-hidden) and description (sets aria-label) per mark and per guide, and hid grid axes from the tree by default when it added them.
   - Zong, Lee, Lundgard, Jang, Hajas and Satyanarayan, EuroVis 2022 (https://vis.csail.mit.edu/pubs/rich-screen-reader-vis-experiences/): three design dimensions, structure, navigation (structural, spatial, targeted) and description (content, composition, verbosity); blind readers want "the global trend, which is why sighted people rely on visualization" rather than a list of encodings; bounded groups help ("a house with many smaller rooms with doors is better than a house with one big room and no doors"); Home and End as anchors; several complementary navigation modes ("spatial felt 'shallow but broad' while targeted felt 'deep but narrow'"). Olli (https://vis.mit.edu/pubs/olli.pdf) implements this as an ARIA tree view (nested lists with group and treeitem roles, adapted from the APG tree example) built from Vega, Vega-Lite and Plot adapters: root overview, then views, then guides (axes and legends), then intervals or categories, then data points.
   - Data Navigator (Elavsky, Nadolskis and Moritz, IEEE VIS 2023, https://dig.cmu.edu/data-navigator/ and https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08475): ARIA on SVG "only provides screen reader access", so it renders "an accessible rendering layer (using semantic HTML) on top of any existing visuals ... This means that interactive elements in a visualization can be real 'button' elements and not an SVG rectangle made from scratch"; navigation rules are edges in a graph, so lists, trees, the two-axis grid used by Highcharts and Visa Chart Components, and spatial structures are all expressible and reachable from keyboard, screen reader, touch, voice and switch inputs.
   - Chartability (https://chartability.fizz.studio/ and https://chartability.github.io/POUR-CAF/): 50 heuristics, 14 critical. Those that bind here: "Low contrast (critical): Geometries and large text must have >3:1 contrast against background, Regular text must have >4.5:1" ("88% of audits we have performed fail this"); "Content is only visual (critical)" with the minimum test matrix JAWS+Chrome, NVDA+Firefox, VoiceOver+Safari on macOS and on iOS; "Small text size (critical): Text (any) must not be smaller than 9pt/12px"; "Color is used alone ... dash patterns (for lines and paths) are required"; "Adjacent elements must have at least 1px white space between them"; "Not CVD-friendly" tested with Viz Palette or the chroma.js palette tool; "Interaction modality only has one input type (critical): Focusing should mirror hovering, selecting (enter or spacebar) should mirror clicks"; "No interaction cues or instructions (critical): All keyboard controls must also be explained"; "Controls override AT controls (critical): Any custom key controls must only apply when the chart or elements have focus"; "Low contrast on interactive elements: the new state should have at least 3:1 contrast against its previous state" or a stroke change of at least 2 px; "Keyboard focus indicator ... must have 4.5:1 contrast against background, must not be fully obscured, and must have at least a 2px border" (stricter than WCAG); "Inappropriate tab stops: Every interactive chart element must NOT have its own tab stop unless the chart is small or the tabs are programmatically revealed"; "Target pointer interaction size is too small: at least 24px x 24px" with alternatives; "No title, summary, or caption (critical)"; and on tables, "A table alone however would also be inaccessible. Providing both can satisfy conflicting accessibility needs for different audiences."
   - Sonification: Chart2Music (https://www.chart2music.com/docs/) adds sonification, generated screen-reader text and arrow-key exploration next to any visual (image, canvas or SVG) through a companion "cc" element, MIT licensed; Apple's Audio Graphs (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/accessibility/audio-graphs) encode "the x-axis as time, and the y-axis as pitch" through AXChart, AXDataSeriesDescriptor and AXLiveAudioGraph, but that is a native accessibility API; no web exposure was found.

19. Announcements: aria-valuetext, live regions and how much is too much.
   - MDN live regions (https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Accessibility/ARIA/Guides/Live_regions): "Normally, only aria-live='polite' is used"; assertive "can be extremely annoying and disruptive and should only be used sparingly"; "Start with an empty live region, then, in a separate step, change the content inside the region" because "Assistive technologies will generally only announce dynamic changes"; aria-atomic="true" makes the whole region re-read (their clock otherwise announces only "34"); role=status and role=log are polite by default; role=timer and marquee are off by default and speak only when focused.
   - Scott O'Hara (https://www.scottohara.me/blog/2022/02/05/are-we-live.html): "browsers and assistive technologies are quirky in how they expose live regions"; injecting a region on demand or toggling it from display:none "is consistently the least supported way"; "ensuring an empty live region exists in your DOM, and injecting content into it when necessary, proves to be the most robust way"; "if you can create an interface that can limit the number of live regions necessary, none being the ideal, then that'd be for the better"; and "a web page with a bunch of live regions that all start barking at assistive technology users at the same time" is as bad as silence.
   - Slider specifics (https://accessibility.build/guides/accessible-slider): "When a slider has focus and its aria-valuenow or aria-valuetext changes, screen readers announce the new value automatically as part of the slider role ... If you also place the value inside an aria-live region, the change is announced twice, which is noisy and confusing"; "The one time a status message helps is when moving the slider changes something elsewhere on the page ... and even then you announce that downstream change, not the slider value itself." The APG had this exact bug: a visible value above the thumb was read twice, once as text and once as aria-valuetext, and the fix (July 2025) was to move the visible value inside the slider element so it is not a separate node (https://github.com/w3c/aria-practices/issues/3257).
   - PhET's AccessibleValueHandler (https://github.com/phetsims/sun/blob/main/js/accessibility/AccessibleValueHandler.ts), the slider base used across their simulations: "Debounce aria-valuetext updates so screen readers announce the latest value instead of speaking every intermediate change when the value updates rapidly without user control. This debounce is only used on scripted changes, not on user input" (ARIA_VALUE_TEXT_DEBOUNCE_DELAY = 300 ms); "During direct user interaction, VoiceOver may announce the pre-update value first, then the debounced value, causing an incorrect first announcement"; it distinguishes the "object response" (aria-valuetext, read "every time the slider value changes") from the "context response" (an alert queued "to only alert after an interaction end", delayed in proportion to how many times the value changed so the value text can finish); and it appends a hair space to force re-announcement when the text is unchanged.
   - Keyboard auto-repeat can deliver "30+ events per second", so expensive side effects (announcement, tooltip) should be debounced while the focus ring moves immediately (https://www.interactive-data-visualization.com/accessible-interactive-data-visualization/keyboard-navigation-patterns/).
   Reading for a film chart: while playing, the playhead is a scripted change, so update aria-valuetext at most every 300 ms and only while the slider has focus; during a drag or a keyboard seek update it immediately; never mirror the value into a live region; use one polite status region, present from first render, for context responses (commit, play, pause, chapter entered) with messages of a few words.

20. Focus rings on SVG.
   - ally.js (https://allyjs.io/tutorials/focusing-in-svg.html): SVG 2 adopted HTML's tabindex; older Firefox and Edge ignored the outline property on SVG elements; g elements "do not have dimensions of their own" so the outline is drawn on the bounding box; WebKit did not render the native outline on SVG links; the recommendation is "Visualize :focus state using styles other than outline"; do not put focusable content in defs, symbol or use, and do not hide SVG that hosts focusable content (Safari tab order breaks).
   - A 2026 field report (https://dev.to/pop3_zxcv/four-things-svg-and-css-did-that-i-did-not-expect-4f4d): Chrome drew the default focus ring on an SVG element at "outline-width: 2.72727px in user units, which is about 24 screen pixels" on a 24-unit-wide canvas, because the ring scales with the viewBox; the author's :focus-visible rule did not cover pointer-set focus, which drew the fat ring.
   - Accessible Data Interfaces (July 2026, https://www.accessible-data-interfaces.com/virtualization-charts-dynamic-data-displays/canvas-svg-chart-keyboard-interaction/): "A browser's default focus outline is drawn in the element's own coordinate space and is frequently clipped by the SVG viewport or by a point near the plot edge. Replace the UA outline with an in-SVG indicator (a thickened stroke plus enlarged r)"; put tabindex on the point, not the path, which stays aria-hidden; roving tabindex (one 0, the rest -1) so the chart is a single tab stop; canvas needs a proxy DOM per point.
   - IDV Guide (June 2026, https://www.interactive-data-visualization.com/accessible-interactive-data-visualization/keyboard-navigation-patterns/): "inside a shadow DOM (web-component charts), focus and aria-activedescendant do not cross the shadow boundary the way you might expect; keep the roving marks and the container in the same tree, and expose a delegatesFocus host so Tab reaches the chart from the light DOM"; "In SVG with transforms, a focused g inherits the transform, so :focus-visible outlines may render in unexpected places"; pass "{ preventScroll: true }" to focus(); "only intercept arrow keys, never Tab itself"; after re-render, re-focus by data identity; open tooltips on focus "positioned so it never covers the focused mark (WCAG 2.4.11)".

## Recommendations for opt-chart

Priority 1 (blocking conformance or the stated plan):

1. Series tokens. Define --op-series-1 to --op-series-6 per theme in oklch() using the Okabe-Ito hue angles (computed here: orange 77, sky blue 236, bluish green 166, yellow 105, blue 244, vermilion 48, reddish purple 346), with two lightness bands per theme and one chroma ceiling (0.14 to 0.16, the largest value in sRGB gamut for every hue at both bands). Starting values: light theme bands L 0.55 and 0.65 on white; dark theme bands L 0.75 and 0.85 on #2B415F if the team accepts an APCA Lc 45 floor for dark, or 0.66 and 0.76 if WCAG 3:1 is the floor. Band assignment from Finding 13: orange, sky blue and reddish purple in one band; bluish green, yellow (rendered as olive) and blue in the other. Default charts to four series (orange, bluish green, blue, reddish purple) in a single band and use six only when needed. Define --op-grid, --op-band and --op-playhead alongside, because 1.4.11 applies to them too.
2. Build-time Rust tests (palette plus farg). For every theme and for each of --op-bg, --op-surface and --op-raised: WCAG ratio >= 3.0 for every series token, the playhead and the band edge; >= 4.5 for every label colour; APCA Lc computed with a pinned port cross-checked against black-on-white (about Lc 106), asserted >= 45 on dark surfaces and reported below 60; pairwise CIEDE2000 >= 10 between all series tokens in normal vision and >= 8 after Brettel simulation of protan, deutan and tritan (report the failing pair and the condition); CIEDE2000 >= 20 from each series token to the surface; a gamut check that each oklch() token round-trips through sRGB without clipping. Keep the thresholds in one documented constant block labelled as project conventions citing Findings 12 and 13, and generate the test data from the tokens, not from a hand-typed list.
3. Stroke widths. Series 2 px; playhead 2 px; band edge 1.5 px only if the band fill itself passes 3:1 or the band is labelled; grid and ticks 1 px are acceptable on the explicit basis that tick labels, the peek readout and the table carry the values (record that reasoning in the test and the docs). Do not raise the grid to 3:1; keep it muted and treat it as ancillary, which is what both the WCAG example and the APCA author allow when values are readable elsewhere.
4. Colour never alone. A distinct stroke-dasharray per series token (solid; 8 4; 2 3; 8 4 2 4; 12 4; 4 2 4 6), a direct end label per series in 4.5:1 text of at least 12 px, marker symbols at sampled points if series are ever drawn without labels, and a 1 px surface-coloured gap wherever a filled band meets a series line or the playhead.
5. Accessibility structure. Replace role=slider on the svg with the following (element names illustrative):
   - opt-chart host: internals.role = "group", aria-labelledby the visible title, aria-describedby the summary paragraph and the key instructions; shadow root created with delegatesFocus.
   - Visible title, a one-paragraph summary (series names, unit, range, number of chapters, the takeaway) and a short instructions line (arrow keys 1 s, Shift+arrow 10 s, Page Up/Down chapter, Home/End, Space play or pause, Escape cancel).
   - svg role="graphics-document" aria-labelledby the title.
   - g aria-hidden="true" containing grid, ticks, band and decorative marks.
   - For each series: g role="graphics-object" aria-label="Name, N samples, min to max unit, from t0 to t1", containing the polyline (aria-hidden) and the end-label text (aria-hidden, because the group already carries the name and group children are otherwise exposed twice).
   - Chapter marks: g role="graphics-object" aria-label="Chapters" containing, per mark, g role="button" tabindex="-1" aria-label="Chapter 2, Boot, 1 minute 20 seconds" with a 24 x 24 px transparent hit rect (roving tabindex if the marks become keyboard reachable).
   - Playhead: g role="slider" tabindex="0" aria-labelledby the title, aria-valuemin="0", aria-valuemax=duration in seconds, aria-valuenow=current seconds, aria-valuetext="1 minute 32 seconds" (with "of 5 minutes 10 seconds" appended only on focus), containing the playhead line, a 24 x 24 px transparent hit rect at the handle, the visible time readout text (inside the slider group so it is not a second node) and the focus-ring rect (stroke currentColor, fill-opacity 0, shown only on :focus-visible).
   - After the svg: div role="status" aria-live="polite" (present from first render, empty) and a details/summary or visible table with th scope headings: time, chapter, one column per series.
   - Never give the host role=slider (children presentational would hide the shadow content), and keep label, description, slider and status in one shadow tree because aria-labelledby cannot cross the boundary.
6. Single-pointer alternative (2.5.7). A pointer press with no movement, on the track or anywhere on the chart, commits a seek to that x on release (the "tap on track" the Understanding text names); previous and next chapter as real buttons in the film's control bar; keep the press-drag pending, commit and cancel choreography for pointer users who drag.
7. Targets (2.5.8). 24 x 24 CSS px invisible hit areas on the handle and on each chapter mark, or prove in the harness that 24 px circles around undersized marks touch no other target; the track itself is one target and needs no change.
8. Focus. Ring drawn as an SVG rect or circle around the thumb: 2 px stroke, 2 px offset, stroke currentColor so it follows forced colours, 3:1 against the surface and a 3:1 change against the unfocused state (2.4.13); :focus-visible only, plus a stronger stroke on the thumb itself; never CSS outline on SVG nodes; delegatesFocus on the host; focus({preventScroll: true}) when moving focus programmatically; check that sticky page chrome never covers the thumb at either end of the track (2.4.11).
9. Reduced motion. Under prefers-reduced-motion: reduce, the playhead still moves (essential) but jumps rather than eases; the ghost, the thumb blend and any easing tied to the --op motion tokens are disabled; reuse the film's existing token switch so the chart and the strip agree.
10. Forced colours. @media (forced-colors: active): series strokes CanvasText (identity carried by dash patterns and end labels), the highlighted or hovered series LinkText, playhead and focus ring Highlight, band fill transparent with a 1 px CanvasText stroke, grid GrayText, labels CanvasText; forced-color-adjust: auto on the svg so currentColor updates; re-run the contrast tests against the emulated light and dark forced palettes.

Priority 2 (quality of the screen-reader and keyboard experience):

11. Announcement policy. Nothing during peek or a pending drag. aria-valuetext updates immediately on keyboard or pointer seeks; while playing, update it at most every 300 ms and only while the slider has focus. The status region receives one short message on commit ("Seeked to 1 minute 32 seconds, chapter Boot"), on play and pause, and when a chapter boundary is crossed while playing (rate-limited to chapters, never per sample). No aria-atomic clock; no assertive regions.
12. Keyboard model. Arrow keys 1 s, Shift+arrow 10 s (or the film's existing J/K/L semantics), Page Up/Down previous and next chapter, Home/End; Space or K toggles play; Escape cancels a pending seek. Intercept these only while the chart has focus (Chartability: no page-level overrides) and never intercept Tab. If series or marks become focusable, use roving tabindex so the chart stays one tab stop, and document the keys in the visible instructions line.
13. Real-render harness (CDP). For each theme (Emulation.setEmulatedMedia with prefers-color-scheme light and dark): (a) screenshots under Emulation.setEmulatedVisionDeficiency for none, deuteranopia, protanopia, tritanopia and achromatopsia, kept as artefacts, with a separability check that the mean CIEDE2000 between the sampled stroke pixels of every two series in the simulated screenshot stays above 8; (b) forced-colors: active with both emulated palettes, asserting every series stroke resolves to a system colour and remains visible against the emulated Canvas; (c) prefers-reduced-motion: reduce, asserting no transition or animation runs on the playhead; (d) CSS.getComputedStyleForNode on every series, grid, band and playhead node, recomputing WCAG and APCA from the computed stroke or fill and the computed surface behind it (not from the tokens), and asserting stroke-width >= 2 for series; (e) Accessibility.getFullAXTree, asserting one slider node with name and value text, one graphics-document, one graphics-object per series with the expected name, no node ignored as a slider descendant, and the status region present; (f) every target's bounding box >= 24 x 24 px or spacing satisfied; (g) the focus ring node visible after Tab with computed stroke width >= 2 px; (h) the single-pointer seek: dispatch a press and release without movement and assert the film time changed.
14. Content. A visible summary with the takeaway, the chapter list and the units (Chartability's critical heuristics; Highcharts' advice against hidden descriptions); the table as the "both" alternative Chartability recommends; no text smaller than 12 px in the chart.

Priority 3 (later):

15. Sonification on the film clock (Web Audio: one instrument track per series, pitch for value, a context tick per chapter, scrubbing tied to the pending seek), following the Highcharts track model; an Olli-style tree view of the data as an alternative navigation structure; CSV download of the table.

## Open questions for the other researchers

- Component API: who owns the slider, opt-film (today: role=slider on its chart) or opt-chart? aria-labelledby, aria-describedby and aria-controls cannot cross a shadow boundary, so the title, summary, instructions, slider and status region must live in one tree, or the host must expose them through ElementInternals (role, ariaLabel, ariaDescription and ariaValueText are reflectable). If opt-chart is a separate element inside opt-film, the film should probably own the slider and the chart should expose only the graphics roles and the table.
- Timeline UX: does a press without movement commit a seek at that x (required for 2.5.7), and does a keyboard seek commit immediately or enter the pending state? What are the step sizes (1 s, 10 s, chapter) and does Escape cancel? Should chapter marks be focusable buttons or only pointer targets with a keyboard equivalent (Page Up/Down)? What does the film already do under prefers-reduced-motion for the thumb blend and the ghost?
- Rendering technology: if any layer moves to canvas or WebGL, every semantic in Recommendation 5 needs an HTML proxy (Data Navigator style) and the focus ring must be painted per frame; stroke rendering should be checked at device pixel ratio 1, where the anti-aliasing loss both WCAG and APCA warn about happens; dash patterns need stroke-linecap decisions so they survive at 2 px.
- Tokens: do --op-series-N, --op-grid, --op-band and --op-playhead join :root through light-dark(), and which surface does the film's chart actually sit on (--op-surface, --op-raised or --op-bg)? Every pair must be tested, and the dark-theme decision (WCAG 3:1 floor versus APCA Lc 45 floor) moves the dark bands from about 0.66 and 0.76 to about 0.75 and 0.85 and therefore changes the whole dark palette.
